# PARC Rice Blast Disease Management Guide

## Official Advisory: Rice Blast Prevention and Control in Pakistan

**Source**: Pakistan Agricultural Research Council (PARC) — Rice Research Division  
**Bulletin Reference**: PARC Rice Advisory Bulletin #35, Updated 2024  
**Applicable Provinces**: Sindh, Punjab (Rice Belt), Khyber Pakhtunkhwa

---

### Disease Identification

**Rice Blast** is caused by the fungal pathogen *Magnaporthe oryzae* (anamorph: *Pyricularia oryzae*). It is the most destructive fungal disease of rice worldwide and causes significant yield losses in Pakistan's rice-growing regions, particularly in Sindh's lower rice belt and Punjab's Kalar tract.

### Symptoms and Field Recognition

Rice blast can infect leaves, stems, nodes, and panicles at different growth stages:

1. **Leaf Blast**: Diamond-shaped or spindle-shaped lesions on leaves, typically 1-3 cm long. Lesions have a gray or whitish center with brown margins. In severe cases, lesions coalesce and the entire leaf dies.
2. **Node Blast**: Black or dark brown discoloration at stem nodes. Infected nodes become brittle and may cause the stem to break (lodging).
3. **Neck Blast (Panicle Blast)**: The most damaging form. Infection at the panicle neck causes the panicle base to turn brown-black. Severe neck blast causes complete panicle death — grains remain unfilled (chaffy). This form can reduce yield by 50-80%.
4. **Collar Blast**: Brown lesions at the junction of the leaf blade and leaf sheath. Causes premature leaf death.

### Conditions Favoring Disease

- High humidity (>80%) and moderate temperatures (20-28°C)
- Prolonged leaf wetness from dew or rain
- Dense canopy from excessive nitrogen fertilization
- Cloudy weather with light intermittent rainfall
- Susceptible varieties grown continuously in the same field

### Approved Fungicide Treatment

**1. Beam 75 WP (Tricyclazole 75%)**
- Dosage: 120 grams per 100 liters of water per acre
- Application: Foliar spray ensuring complete coverage of leaves and panicles
- Timing: Apply preventatively at late tillering stage if weather conditions favor blast. For curative application, spray immediately upon first symptom appearance.
- Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 21 days
- Mode of Action: Melanin biosynthesis inhibitor — prevents the fungus from penetrating plant cells
- Important: Maximum 120 grams per acre per application. Tricyclazole is most effective as a preventative measure. Do not exceed recommended dosage.

**2. Nativo 75 WG (Tebuconazole 50% + Trifloxystrobin 25%)**
- Dosage: 65 grams per 100 liters of water per acre
- Timing: Apply at booting to heading stage for panicle blast protection
- Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 21 days
- Advantage: Dual mode of action provides both systemic and contact protection

**3. Amistar Top 325 SC (Azoxystrobin 20% + Difenoconazole 12.5%)**
- Dosage: 200 ml per 100 liters of water per acre
- Timing: Apply at first sign of leaf blast or as preventative spray during heading
- Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 14 days
- Advantage: Also provides protection against brown spot disease

### Spray Application for Rice

- Spray during calm weather — wind causes drift and poor coverage
- Spray in the early morning after dew dries or in the late afternoon
- Do not spray if rain is expected within 4-6 hours — fungicide needs time to dry and be absorbed
- For paddy fields, drain water to knee-deep level before spraying for better access
- Use 100-120 liters of water per acre for thorough coverage
- Direct spray at the upper canopy where new growth is most vulnerable

### Organic and Biological Control

1. **Trichoderma Seed Treatment**: Treat rice seed with *Trichoderma viride* at 4 grams per kilogram of seed before nursery sowing. Mix the biofungicide powder with seed in a dry container, shake well, and allow treated seed to dry in shade before sowing. Trichoderma colonizes the seed surface and root zone, providing biological protection against blast.

2. **Silicon Foliar Spray**: Dissolve 2 ml potassium silicate per liter of water and spray every 10 days starting from the tillering stage. Silicon strengthens the outer cell walls (epidermis) of rice leaves, creating a physical barrier that reduces fungal penetration. This is particularly effective as a preventative measure.

3. **Compost Tea Spray**: Prepare mature compost tea by soaking well-decomposed compost in water for 3-5 days. Strain and spray on foliage. Contains beneficial microorganisms that compete with blast fungus on the leaf surface.

### Cultural Practices for Blast Prevention

1. **Nitrogen Management**: Excessive nitrogen fertilization is the single most important cultural factor promoting rice blast. Apply nitrogen in split doses as recommended by soil test results. Never exceed 120 kg urea per acre.

2. **Water Management**: Maintain continuous flooding during vegetative growth to reduce leaf wetness fluctuation. Alternate wetting and drying (AWD) during reproductive stage helps reduce humidity in the canopy.

3. **Resistant Varieties**: Plant PARC-recommended blast-resistant varieties including IRRI-6, Super Basmati (moderate resistance), KSK-434, and Shaheen Basmati. Varietal resistance combined with good agronomic practices is the most sustainable management strategy.

4. **Field Sanitation**: Remove and destroy rice stubble and straw after harvest. The blast fungus survives on crop residue between seasons. Burning or deep incorporation of stubble reduces inoculum for the next crop.

5. **Planting Date**: Follow recommended sowing dates for your region. Late-planted rice is more vulnerable to blast during the cooler autumn months.

### Brown Spot Disease (Bipolaris oryzae)

Rice brown spot, caused by *Bipolaris oryzae*, often occurs alongside blast. Symptoms include oval brown spots with gray centers on leaves. Brown spot is typically associated with nutrient-deficient soils, particularly low potassium and zinc. Management includes:
- Balanced fertilization with adequate potassium and zinc sulfate
- Foliar sprays of Amistar Top 325 SC (200 ml/acre) also control brown spot
- Seed treatment with fungicide or Trichoderma before nursery sowing
