# PARC Wheat Rust Disease Management Bulletin

## Official Advisory: Wheat Leaf Rust and Stripe Rust Management in Pakistan

**Source**: Pakistan Agricultural Research Council (PARC) — Crop Protection Division  
**Bulletin Reference**: PARC Wheat Advisory Bulletin #42, Updated 2024  
**Applicable Provinces**: Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Balochistan

---

### Disease Identification

**Wheat Leaf Rust (Brown Rust)** is caused by the fungal pathogen *Puccinia triticina*. It is the most prevalent wheat disease across Punjab and Sindh. The disease appears as small, circular to oval, orange-brown pustules (uredia) scattered randomly on the upper surface of leaves. As infection progresses, pustules turn dark brown to black (telia stage).

**Wheat Stripe Rust (Yellow Rust)** is caused by *Puccinia striiformis* f. sp. *tritici*. It produces yellow to orange pustules arranged in distinct linear stripes along the leaf veins. Stripe rust is more common in cooler regions and at higher altitudes, particularly in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and northern Punjab during February-March.

### Symptoms and Field Recognition

Farmers should inspect wheat leaves regularly during the tillering to heading stage (January-March). Key symptoms include:

1. **Leaf Rust**: Random circular orange-brown spots on leaf surface, each 1-2 mm diameter. Rubbing the leaf releases orange spore powder on fingers. Lower leaves are affected first, progressing upward.
2. **Stripe Rust**: Yellow parallel lines of pustules following leaf veins. Infection often starts from leaf tips. Entire leaf may turn yellow and dry prematurely if untreated.
3. **Yield Impact**: Severe leaf rust can reduce grain yield by 20-30%. Stripe rust epidemics can cause up to 60% yield loss in susceptible varieties.

### Approved Chemical Treatment

The following fungicides are registered and approved by the Department of Plant Protection (DPP), Government of Pakistan, for wheat rust management:

**1. Nativo 75 WG (Tebuconazole 50% + Trifloxystrobin 25%)**
- Dosage: 65 grams per 100 liters of water per acre
- Application: Foliar spray using knapsack or tractor-mounted boom sprayer
- Timing: Apply at first appearance of rust pustules. Repeat after 15-20 days if disease pressure continues.
- Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 21 days before harvest
- Mode of Action: Systemic + contact. Provides both curative and preventative control.
- Important: Do not exceed 65 grams per acre. Higher doses do not improve efficacy and increase chemical residue risk.

**2. Amistar Top 325 SC (Azoxystrobin 20% + Difenoconazole 12.5%)**
- Dosage: 200 ml per 100 liters of water per acre
- Application: Foliar spray with uniform coverage on both upper and lower leaf surfaces
- Timing: Apply preventatively at first sign of disease or when weather conditions favor rust development (cool nights, morning dew, moderate temperatures 15-22°C)
- Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 14 days before harvest

**3. Tilt 250 EC (Propiconazole 25%)**
- Dosage: 200 ml per 100 liters of water per acre
- Timing: Curative application within 48 hours of symptom appearance
- Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 30 days before harvest
- Note: Tilt is most effective when applied early. Efficacy decreases significantly once more than 20% leaf area is infected.

### Organic and Cultural Control Methods

For farmers preferring organic or low-cost alternatives:

1. **Neem Oil Emulsion**: Mix 5 ml pure neem oil and 2 grams liquid soap per liter of water. Spray on affected leaves every 7 days. Neem oil acts as a preventative measure and suppresses early-stage spore germination.

2. **Baking Soda Solution**: Dissolve 10 grams baking soda (sodium bicarbonate) and 5 ml liquid soap in 1 liter water. Spray weekly. Creates an alkaline leaf surface environment hostile to fungal spore germination.

3. **Resistant Varieties**: Plant PARC-recommended rust-resistant wheat varieties including Faisalabad-2008, Galaxy-2013, Zincol-2016, and Akbar-2019. Variety resistance is the most cost-effective long-term strategy.

4. **Cultural Practices**: Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization which produces lush, susceptible foliage. Maintain recommended plant spacing for air circulation. Remove volunteer wheat plants that serve as disease bridges between seasons.

### Spray Application Guidelines

- Spray in the early morning after dew has dried, or in the late afternoon
- Do not spray during rain or when rain is expected within 4-6 hours
- Ensure complete leaf coverage — both upper and lower surfaces
- Use clean water for mixing; hard or alkaline water reduces fungicide efficacy
- Wear protective gloves and face mask during application
- Observe the Pre-Harvest Interval strictly before grain harvest

### When to Seek Expert Help

If more than 30% of the leaf area is infected, contact your nearest Agricultural Extension Office immediately. Severe infections may require area-wide coordinated spraying to prevent epidemic spread to neighboring farms.
