# PARC Cotton Leaf Curl Virus (CLCuV) Management Guide

## Official Advisory: Cotton Leaf Curl Virus Disease and Whitefly Vector Control

**Source**: Pakistan Agricultural Research Council (PARC) — Cotton Research Division  
**Bulletin Reference**: PARC Cotton Advisory Bulletin #28, Updated 2024  
**Applicable Provinces**: Punjab (Southern), Sindh

---

### Disease Identification

**Cotton Leaf Curl Virus (CLCuV)** is caused by a complex of Begomovirus species transmitted exclusively by the whitefly vector *Bemisia tabaci*. CLCuV is the most devastating disease of cotton in Pakistan, responsible for billions of rupees in annual losses. The disease does not spread through direct contact, seed, or soil — only through whitefly feeding.

### Symptoms and Field Recognition

1. **Early Stage**: Upward curling of young leaves at the top of the plant. Leaf margins roll inward. Small vein thickening (enation) visible on the underside of affected leaves.
2. **Progressive Stage**: Severe leaf distortion and cupping. Dark green coloration of affected leaves. Development of leaf-like outgrowths (enations) on the lower leaf surface along veins.
3. **Advanced Stage**: Stunted plant growth. Reduced boll formation. Plants become bushy with shortened internodes. Severely affected plants may produce no harvestable cotton.
4. **Whitefly Detection**: Turn the leaf over and tap gently — small white flying insects (1-2 mm) indicate whitefly presence. Honeydew secretion creates a sticky, shiny surface on lower leaves. Black sooty mold may develop on honeydew deposits.

### Critical Control Strategy: Target the Whitefly Vector

Since CLCuV is a viral disease, there is no direct chemical cure for the virus itself. Management focuses entirely on controlling the whitefly vector to prevent virus transmission.

**Action Threshold**: Begin insecticide application when whitefly population reaches 5 adults per leaf on the upper canopy.

### Approved Insecticides for Whitefly Control

**1. Imidacloprid 200 SL (Confidor)**
- Active Ingredient: Imidacloprid 20%
- Dosage: 250 ml per 100 liters of water per acre
- Application: Foliar spray targeting the undersides of leaves where whitefly adults and nymphs feed
- Timing: Apply when whitefly count reaches 5 per leaf. Best results when sprayed in early morning or late evening when whitefly is less mobile.
- Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 14 days
- Important: Maximum 250 ml per acre. Excessive use accelerates whitefly resistance development. Rotate with different chemical classes.

**2. Acetamiprid 20 SP (Mospilan)**
- Active Ingredient: Acetamiprid 20%
- Dosage: 100 grams per 100 liters of water per acre
- Timing: Early morning application for maximum contact with resting whiteflies
- Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 7 days
- Advantage: Shorter PHI allows application closer to harvest. Lower toxicity to beneficial insects compared to older insecticides.

**3. Diafenthiuron 50 WP (Polo)**
- Active Ingredient: Diafenthiuron 50%
- Dosage: 200 grams per 100 liters of water per acre
- Timing: Evening application preferred — Diafenthiuron is activated by sunlight after application
- Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 14 days
- Mode of Action: Inhibits mitochondrial ATP synthesis in whitefly. Effective against both adults and nymphs.

### Resistance Management Protocol

To prevent whitefly from developing insecticide resistance, follow the PARC-recommended rotation protocol:
- Week 1-2: Neonicotinoid group (Imidacloprid or Acetamiprid)
- Week 3-4: Diafenthiuron (different mode of action)
- Week 5-6: Return to neonicotinoid or use pyriproxyfen (growth regulator)

Never spray the same chemical class more than twice consecutively.

### Organic and Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

1. **Yellow Sticky Traps**: Install yellow plastic sheets (30×25 cm) coated with petroleum jelly or commercial sticky adhesive at canopy height. Place one trap every 10 feet along field borders. Yellow color specifically attracts whiteflies. Replace traps when fully covered.

2. **Neem Oil Spray**: Mix 5 ml pure neem oil per liter of water with a small amount of liquid soap as emulsifier. Spray weekly on leaf undersides. Neem oil disrupts whitefly feeding behavior and reduces egg-laying.

3. **Resistant Varieties**: Plant PARC-approved CLCuV-resistant cotton varieties including FH-142, MNH-886, and NIAB-878. These varieties show tolerance to the virus and maintain reasonable yield even under moderate whitefly pressure.

4. **Cultural Practices**:
   - Remove and destroy CLCuV-infected plants early in the season to reduce virus inoculum
   - Maintain clean field borders — remove alternate host weeds (especially *Sida* species)
   - Avoid late planting — early sown cotton (April-May) escapes peak whitefly populations
   - Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization which attracts whitefly to lush green foliage

### Companion Planting and Biological Control

- Plant maize or sorghum as border crops — these act as windbreaks reducing whitefly migration
- Conserve natural enemies: ladybird beetles, lacewings, and *Encarsia formosa* parasitic wasps are effective whitefly predators
- Avoid broad-spectrum insecticide spraying that kills beneficial insects

### Important Warnings

- Do NOT spray pyrethroids (cypermethrin, deltamethrin) for whitefly control — they are ineffective against whitefly and kill beneficial predators, leading to whitefly population resurgence
- Never mix two insecticides together unless specifically recommended by an extension officer
- Always use clean, fresh water for spray preparation
